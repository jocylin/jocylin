Part 01

What Is STRC

STRC (Strategy Stretch) is a perpetual preferred stock issued by Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy), listed on Nasdaq. Dividends are paid monthly in cash; the rate resets monthly to encourage trading near the $100 par value.

The essence: high-yield fixed income with embedded BTC optionality. Saylor monetizes BTC's volatility — using the corporate structure as a bridge to convert future BTC appreciation into current cash dividends for investors.

Five Structural Risks

1

Buyer Concentration Risk

STRC's marginal buyers are BTC bulls. In a bear market, BTC bulls panic and reduce exposure — the same moment STRC buyers disappear is the exact moment you need liquidity most.

2

Dividend Is Deferrable (Not Hard Debt)

STRC is preferred equity, not debt. The legal structure allows Strategy to defer dividends without triggering default — fundamentally different from the statutory obligation of a U.S. Treasury.

3

Refinancing Cliff (2027–2028)

Approximately $7B in convertible notes mature in 2027–2028. If BTC is depressed at that point, refinancing capacity is constrained and the entire capital structure comes under pressure.

4

Single-Factor Correlation

STRC is highly correlated to BTC. If you already hold BTC, adding STRC provides no real diversification — it's a leveraged enhancement of the same bet.

5

The Network Effect Moat of Treasuries

U.S. Treasuries = global dollar settlement = repo market collateral = the foundation of all financial activity. This 70-year self-reinforcing cycle cannot be replicated by any single issuer's credibility.

"You don't know a tool's true properties until it survives a deep bear market."
— The fundamental disposition toward any instrument that hasn't been cycle-tested

Part 04

Comparative Analysis

STRC vs. Short-Term Treasuries

🪙 STRC

  • ⚠ Issuer: Strategy (single BTC-holding company)
  • ✓ Annual yield: 11.5%
  • ✗ Liquidity: secondary market, thin in bear cycles
  • ✗ Principal protection: no mandatory redemption
  • ✓ Transparency: BTC holdings on-chain verifiable
  • ✗ Bear market behavior: highly correlated to BTC

🏛 Short-Term U.S. Treasuries

  • ✓ Issuer: U.S. Government (tax-backed obligation)
  • ⚠ Annual yield: 4-5%
  • ✓ Liquidity: deepest in the world
  • ✓ Principal protection: statutory maturity repayment
  • ✓ Network effect: repo market base collateral
  • ✓ Bear market behavior: safe-haven, typically appreciates
❌ Misidentifying the Function Is the Biggest Risk

The core function of short-term Treasuries is the ability to sell at near par value even in the worst market conditions. STRC cannot do this. Treating STRC as a cash equivalent is a functional misclassification — the risk is in how you use it, not in the instrument itself.

Six Core Takeaways

🎯
Understand the Function
STRC = a BTC credit derivative. How much you hold should reflect how much single-factor BTC risk you're willing to carry.
🌊
Liquidity Stress Test
"I'll just sell when I need cash" fails in a bear market. A truly safe asset is one that still has buyers at the worst possible moment.
🔐
The Moat Is Real
$2.25B cash + no-margin-call convertibles make near-term default extremely unlikely. But a 3-year BTC bear is outside that safety window.
🌐
Endgame Narrative
STRC is a transitional product. Once BTC becomes a true reserve asset, holding BTC itself may be closer to "digital sovereign debt" than any BTC-backed wrapper.
📊
ETH Treasury Discipline
ATM below MNAV is dilution, not accumulation. SharpLink understands what Saylor is actually doing. BMNR does not.
🧠
Sense of Proportion > Trust
Giving Saylor 9/10 credibility is reasonable. But good portfolio construction never depends on any single entity's reputation — even a 9/10.

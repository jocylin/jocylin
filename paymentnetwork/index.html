01

Why now

Three independent shifts have opened a window that did not exist eighteen months ago.

Regulation

A live regulatory perimeter

Hong Kong's Stablecoins Ordinance took effect in August 2025, and the LEAP framework now covers stablecoins, virtual-asset dealing, and tokenized real-world assets end to end. With only a handful of licenses expected initially, coordination — not scale alone — is what gets companies to the front of the queue.

Market

A deal-flow peak

Crypto-payment companies are raising and exiting at record valuations across card issuing, cross-border PSP, and tokenization rails. The category has momentum, capital, and live revenue — but no shared, neutral home in Asia.

Structure

An organizer vacuum

The most credible neutral conveners of cross-border settlement have stepped back, leaving the multilateral middle empty. The opening is for a privately organized, compliance-first commercial rail — not another single-bloc platform.

The opening

Today, not one of these companies sits inside a multilateral, neutral, Asia-organized alliance. That is the space this network is built to occupy.

02

What the network does

Four functions, each designed to be useful to members from the first quarter — not a conference, and not a standards body without a rail.

Function 01

Convene

A closed-door working group of founding members meeting quarterly, plus an annual Hong Kong summit. A room where the people building Asia's payment rails actually set the agenda together.

Function 02

Interconnect

Members integrate with one another — card issuing meeting cross-border channels, tokenized assets settling over stablecoin rails, compliance tooling shared across PSPs. The network is the matchmaker.

Function 03

Represent

A single, collective voice in dialogue with Hong Kong regulators and policy bodies. Engaging on framework and use-case clarity is far cheaper and more credible as a group than company by company.

Function 04

Research

A quarterly Asia payment research report and a maintained member directory — shared market intelligence that raises the standing of every participant.

03

Design principles

How the alliance is structured to stay neutral and worth belonging to. These are the founding design commitments — refined together with the founding cohort, not fixed terms.

P1

Contribution-linked economics

The alliance is designed so that economic value generated at the shared settlement layer is allocated by contribution to the network, rather than captured solely by an issuer. The intent is structural alignment among members; specific mechanics are developed with the founding cohort.

P2

Non-discriminatory pricing

Members are treated on equal pricing terms regardless of size, following the cooperative model used by established clearing utilities. No member buys advantage simply by being large.

P3

Jurisdiction-neutral governance

The board is designed so that no single jurisdiction can dominate. Neutrality is the asset; the alliance is deliberately not built as any one bloc's instrument.

P4

Compliance-first, chain-agnostic

A Western-compliance posture and admission control are built in from the start, and the network is not tied to any single chain or issuer. Real assets and real rails, not a logo collection.

04

What founding partners get — and give

As a founding partner, you

  • Help write the governance — board structure, admission rules, and economic design are set with the founding cohort, not handed down.
  • Sit at the quarterly working group and shape the annual summit agenda.
  • Gain cross-member integration — warm introductions and technical interconnection with other anchors.
  • Join the collective regulatory dialogue in Hong Kong as part of a recognized group.
  • Receive founding recognition in the network's research and public identity.

In return, we ask partners to

  • Commit a named senior representative to the working group.
  • Engage in good faith on interconnection with other members where it makes commercial sense.
  • Uphold the alliance's compliance and admission standards.
  • Help refine the founding charter during the first two quarters.
On the organizer

Asia Payment Network is convened by IOSG Ventures, an investment firm active in digital-asset and payment infrastructure since 2017.

IOSG's role is deliberately bounded: to organize, to research, and to invest in the equity of key companies. The alliance is member-shaped, and IOSG holds no license and operates no payment business of its own. The aim is a network that outlasts and outgrows any single organizer.

The founding cohort is small by design.

We're holding the first cohort to six to ten anchor members so governance stays workable and every founding voice carries weight. If your company builds payment, settlement, or tokenization rails in or into Asia, we'd like to talk.
